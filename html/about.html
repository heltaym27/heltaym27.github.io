About Site

The story

Cognate is an undergraduate-run archive and blog for members of the University of Leicester Psychology Society. Its purpose is simple but ambitious: to make psychological science more accessible without losing academic rigour. All publications here are created by psychology undergraduates and shaped by an editor-based review process involving both students and academics. This undergraduate peer-reviewed approach ensures that articles meet high standards of clarity, accuracy and critical thinking while remaining engaging for a wider audience. The project was founded by Helena Tatay Marco, a psychology undergraduate passionate about improving how science is communicated in the media. Too often, important research stays locked behind jargon and paywalls, inaccessible to anyone outside academia. This platform aims to bridge that gap by translating complex findings into clear, thoughtful, and responsible science communication. At its core, this is a student-led initiative. If it runs smoothly and proves useful within the society, the hope is that more members will get involved—contributing articles, joining the editorial team, or helping shape the project's direction. In the long term, the goal is for the university and wider academic community to take an interest and support the growth of this space as a sustainable hub for accessible, high-quality psychology communication. Cognate is named for its meaning: “related by origin or nature.” Reflecting the site’s mission to connect academic psychology with public understanding.
